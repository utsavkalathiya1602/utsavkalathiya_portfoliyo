Working Remotely as a Developer: Habits That Make Async Teams Work

By Utsav Kalathiya · 7 Oct 2026 · 3 min read

Since May 2026 I've worked as a remote Full Stack Developer at Elpiora, a product-based startup, building features across React, Node.js, Express and MongoDB with product managers and designers who aren't in the same room, and often aren't online at the same time.

Remote work is now a normal way for software teams to operate, and the hardest part isn't the code. It's making collaboration work when you can't tap someone on the shoulder. These are the habits that make asynchronous ("async") teamwork run smoothly, and that I'd recommend to any developer going remote.

1. If it isn't written down, it didn't happen

Decisions made in a private chat or a quick call disappear. Put them where the work lives: in the ticket, the pull request, or the design document. Anyone joining later, including you in three months, can then see what was decided and why without having to ask.

2. Write pull requests people can review without you

In an async team, your PR description is the conversation. A good one answers four questions:

  • What changed, in one or two sentences.
  • Why: the problem or ticket it solves.
  • How to test it: the steps a reviewer can follow.
  • What it looks like: screenshots or a short recording for any UI change.

A reviewer in another time zone can then approve it in one pass instead of a day of back-and-forth questions.

3. Keep pull requests small

Small, focused PRs get reviewed quickly; huge ones sit in the queue and collect merge conflicts. Splitting a feature into a few reviewable steps (data model, then API, then UI) keeps work moving even when reviewers are only available for short windows.

4. Make status updates that unblock people

A useful async update fits in three lines:

Done:    order history API + tests
Next:    connect it to the React order page
Blocked: need the final empty-state design

The "blocked" line is the most important one. Raise blockers early and specifically, because in an async team a blocker you mention tomorrow can cost a full day.

5. Ask questions that can be answered in one reply

"Hey, got a minute?" followed by silence wastes a round-trip. Instead, send the whole question at once: the context, what you've already tried, and the specific decision you need. You'll usually get a complete answer in a single message.

6. Know when to switch to a call

Async is the default, not a rule. If a thread has gone back and forth two or three times without converging, a ten-minute call is faster. Afterwards, post a short written summary of what was decided, so the outcome is back in the written record.

7. Protect focus time and overlap time

Remote work makes deep focus easier, as long as you guard it. Block uninterrupted time for complex work, batch your message-checking, and keep a predictable window when teammates know they can reach you for quick questions.

8. Set boundaries

When your office is also your home, the workday can quietly expand. A clear start and end, notifications off after hours, and a proper break make you more productive over months, not just days.

Takeaway

Async work rewards developers who communicate clearly and proactively in writing. Write decisions down, make PRs easy to review, surface blockers early and ask complete questions. These habits make remote teams fast, and they make you a better teammate in any office, too.
